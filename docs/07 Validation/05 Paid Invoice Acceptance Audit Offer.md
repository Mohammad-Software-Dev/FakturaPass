# FakturaPass Paid Invoice Acceptance Audit Offer

**Working German name:** FakturaPass Rechnungsannahme-Check  
**English reference name:** FakturaPass Invoice Acceptance Audit  
**Status:** Pre-product commercial hypothesis  
**Iteration:** 3  
**Purpose:** Validate willingness to pay and deliver value before building the full SaaS

## 1. Offer in one sentence

> We analyze how invoices leave your existing ERP, test representative invoices against applicable technical and known recipient requirements, identify preventable rejection risks, and provide a bounded path to reliable structured invoicing—without requiring an ERP replacement.

## 2. Why this offer exists

The audit is not merely a lead magnet. It is the first sellable FakturaPass service and must answer four questions:

1. Is the customer's problem costly and urgent enough to pay for?
2. Can the workflow be solved through a reusable product pattern rather than unlimited consulting?
3. Which source mapping and recipient profiles should the product support first?
4. Will the customer proceed to a recurring pilot after seeing the findings?

The audit creates value even if FakturaPass is not a fit. The customer receives a documented view of its current process, concrete risks, prioritized corrections, and realistic implementation options.

## 3. Ideal audit customer

The offer is intended for a German B2B supplier that:

- has approximately 20–200 employees;
- sends roughly 200–5,000 outbound invoices per month;
- uses a legacy, customized, vertical, or internally built ERP or billing system;
- serves public bodies or larger enterprises;
- has experienced invoice rejection, manual portal entry, payment delay, or uncertainty about customer-specific requirements;
- can provide a stable invoice export and representative artifacts;
- wants to preserve the existing source system.

Strong triggers include a customer ending PDF acceptance, a public-sector contract, a recent rejection escalation, an expensive ERP proposal, or an active 2027/2028 readiness project.

## 4. Customer outcomes

By the end of the audit, the customer should understand:

- how invoice data currently moves from the ERP to the recipient;
- whether the provided data can support XRechnung or ZUGFeRD generation;
- which required fields are missing, malformed, or mapped incorrectly;
- which failures are standards-related and which are recipient-specific;
- which corrective actions belong in the ERP, mapping layer, recipient profile, or human workflow;
- which input, output, and delivery path is realistic for production;
- what remains uncertain and must be confirmed with a tax adviser, recipient, or software vendor;
- whether a FakturaPass pilot is commercially and technically sensible.

## 5. Standard scope

The initial fixed-scope audit includes:

### Customer environment

- one German legal entity;
- one ERP, billing system, or stable export pattern;
- one current invoice-generation workflow;
- up to five important recipients;
- up to 50 representative invoices or invoice records;
- up to five rejection messages, portal errors, or recipient rulebooks;
- one kickoff workshop and one findings workshop.

### Supported input for the audit

At least one of:

- CSV export;
- JSON export;
- documented REST API payload;
- structured XML already generated by the source system;
- PDF samples accompanied by authoritative source data.

PDF-only analysis may identify candidate fields but cannot establish that extracted values are legally authoritative without customer confirmation.

### Initial target outputs

- XRechnung UBL;
- ZUGFeRD using an appropriate EN 16931 profile;
- another output only when explicitly agreed and feasible within the audit.

### Validation layers

Where relevant and supported, the audit evaluates:

1. source-file integrity and parseability;
2. presence and consistency of invoice header and line data;
3. mathematical and tax-total consistency;
4. output schema requirements;
5. EN 16931 business rules;
6. applicable XRechnung or ZUGFeRD rules;
7. known recipient references and routing requirements;
8. attachment and filename conventions;
9. the proposed delivery and response workflow.

The exact rule versions and tools used must be recorded in the final report.

## 6. Required customer inputs

Before work begins, the customer provides:

- a named business owner and technical contact;
- a description of the current invoice workflow;
- the relevant export specification or sample payload;
- 20–50 representative, anonymized invoice records;
- at least one known-good invoice if available;
- examples of rejected or delayed invoices if available;
- rejection messages and portal feedback;
- instructions from up to five important recipients;
- expected formats and delivery channels;
- confirmation of which supplied values are authoritative;
- permission to process the supplied material for the audit.

The customer must remove unnecessary personal data and secrets before transfer. FakturaPass must never request production credentials, private keys, live authentication tokens, or unrestricted system access for a discovery audit.

## 8. Delivery process

### Step 1: Qualification call — 30 minutes

Confirm:

- target segment fit;
- invoice volume and customer complexity;
- recent failures or active deadline;
- data and artifact availability;
- decision-maker access;
- expected audit outcome;
- willingness to pay.

Do not sell the audit when the problem is hypothetical, already solved, or outside the supported scope.

### Step 2: Written scope and data checklist

Send a short proposal specifying:

- legal entity, system, recipients, and sample size;
- deliverables;
- price and payment schedule;
- timeline starting only after complete input receipt;
- confidentiality and data-handling terms;
- assumptions and exclusions;
- criteria for a scope change.

### Step 3: Kickoff workshop — 60–90 minutes

Walk through one invoice from source to acceptance. Confirm terminology, authoritative data, known failures, recipient priorities, and owners.

### Step 4: Artifact intake and safety check

- inventory all files;
- confirm anonymization;
- reject secrets or unnecessary personal data;
- record permission and retention period;
- hash or otherwise identify the exact analyzed artifacts;
- store material in a customer-specific restricted location.

### Step 5: Mapping and validation analysis

- profile the input structure;
- map representative fields into a canonical invoice model;
- calculate or verify totals deterministically;
- generate test output where feasible;
- run documented official or established validation artifacts;
- apply recipient-specific checks supported by evidence;
- record every ambiguity rather than guessing.

### Step 6: Findings review — 60 minutes

Present:

- the current workflow;
- most important risks;
- preventable failure examples;
- recommended corrections;
- production options;
- whether a FakturaPass pilot is justified.

### Step 7: Final package and decision

Deliver the agreed files and request a concrete decision:

- proceed to a paid pilot;
- complete customer-side prerequisites and reconsider;
- use another solution;
- stop because the economic or technical case is weak.

## 10. Pricing hypothesis

### Discovery price bands

| Offer | Price hypothesis | Intended use |
|---|---:|---|
| Founding-customer audit | €500 plus VAT | First 1–3 customers; tightly scoped; explicit feedback commitment |
| Standard audit | €1,000 plus VAT | One system, up to five recipients, normal artifact quality |

This credit should not turn the audit into free presales work. The customer receives a useful standalone result whether or not a pilot follows.

### Payment terms hypothesis

- 50% at booking;
- 50% when the findings package is delivered;
- work begins after the initial payment and complete inputs;
- external provider fees require prior customer approval;
- additional scope requires a written change agreement.

For the first few audits, upfront payment in full may be operationally simpler.

## 12. Scope boundaries

The standard audit does not include:

- production deployment;
- unrestricted access to the customer's ERP;
- complete bookkeeping, accounts payable, tax calculation, or legal archiving;
- tax or legal advice;
- certification of legal compliance;
- guaranteed recipient acceptance or payment;
- Peppol access-point certification;
- support for every EU jurisdiction;
- mass OCR conversion of historical PDFs;
- manual correction of the customer's entire invoice population;
- unlimited recipient research;
- creation of missing authoritative values without customer confirmation;
- penetration testing, formal security audit, or SLA commitment.

If a requested outcome falls outside these boundaries, either issue a clearly priced change order or decline it.

## 13. Responsibility model

### FakturaPass is responsible for

- analyzing only the agreed artifacts and workflow;
- recording validation tools, rule versions, assumptions, and uncertainties;
- protecting provided data according to the agreed handling terms;
- delivering the agreed assessment and recommendations;
- distinguishing technical findings from tax or legal questions;
- deleting or returning customer artifacts according to the retention agreement.

### The customer is responsible for

- providing accurate, authorized, and appropriately anonymized materials;
- confirming authoritative commercial and tax data;
- confirming private recipient instructions and contractual requirements;
- obtaining tax or legal advice where required;
- deciding and implementing changes to its ERP and business process;
- testing and approving any later production workflow.

### Third parties are responsible for

- their own platforms, networks, portals, and recipient systems;
- maintaining their published requirements and service availability;
- providing acknowledgements and rejection feedback where supported.

## 14. Data protection and security baseline

Before selling the first audit, establish at minimum:

- a simple data-processing and confidentiality agreement appropriate to the role;
- an approved EU or German storage location;
- per-customer access separation;
- encryption in transit and at rest;
- a short default artifact retention period;
- a deletion checklist and record;
- a list of subprocessors;
- a breach and incident contact path;
- a rule prohibiting production secrets and unnecessary personal data;
- an anonymization guide for customers.

The exact GDPR roles depend on the engagement and should be reviewed by qualified German counsel or a data-protection professional before processing real customer invoice data at scale.

## 15. Quality checklist

The audit is not complete until:

- [ ] scope and assumptions match the signed proposal;
- [ ] all analyzed artifacts are inventoried;
- [ ] sensitive or unnecessary data has been handled according to policy;
- [ ] at least one representative workflow is traced end to end;
- [ ] each technical finding references a reproducible rule or test;
- [ ] recipient requirements include a source and review date;
- [ ] ambiguous values are labeled and not silently guessed;
- [ ] recommended changes identify an owner;
- [ ] the report distinguishes standards, recipient rules, and internal policy;
- [ ] the pilot recommendation is bounded;
- [ ] retention or deletion actions are scheduled;
- [ ] the customer has received and discussed the findings.

## 18. Conversion to a pilot

The audit should lead to a pilot only when the evidence supports a repeatable path.

### Pilot entry criteria

- stable input contract agreed;
- required fields available or customer-owned remediation scheduled;
- initial output format selected;
- no more than five recipient profiles in the first phase;
- validation scope and rule versions identified;
- delivery path selected;
- business and technical owners assigned;
- test corpus approved;
- security and data-processing requirements accepted;
- success criteria and price agreed.

### Pilot success metrics

- percentage of representative invoices passing all configured preflight layers;
- first-pass recipient acceptance where observable;
- number and type of manual corrections;
- time from source export to accepted invoice;
- support minutes per 100 invoices;
- prevented repeat failures;
- reusable mappings and recipient rules created;
- customer decision to continue as a paid subscriber.

### Pilot commercial hypothesis

- onboarding: €1,500–€5,000 depending on mapping and recipient scope;
- subscription: €149–€399 per month plus volume or profile tiers;
- audit fee credited to onboarding;
- no unpaid custom connector work.

These amounts remain hypotheses until tested in real sales conversations.

## 19. Internal learning capture

After every audit, record:

- acquisition source and sales-cycle length;
- qualification score;
- price proposed and price accepted;
- hours spent by activity;
- external provider cost;
- source-system pattern;
- missing and ambiguous fields;
- recipient rules discovered;
- validation failures by layer;
- reusable mapping and test assets;
- customer objections;
- reasons the customer did or did not proceed;
- expected recurring revenue;
- work that was bespoke and should not enter the product.

Do not place confidential customer content in the general learning log. Store only anonymized patterns with permission.

## 20. Audit validation thresholds

Continue offering and productizing the audit when:

1. at least three qualified prospects accept a paid audit or equivalent commitment;
2. at least one customer pays the proposed price without requiring unlimited scope;
3. the same mapping or recipient-rule pattern appears across at least three organizations;
4. delivery can be completed in 20–30 founder hours or less after early learning;
5. at least half of completed audits produce a credible paid-pilot opportunity;
6. customers value actionable preflight and evidence beyond generic conversion;
7. the estimated recurring subscription supports ongoing standards and support costs.

Revise or stop the offer when:

- prospects want only free advice;
- every engagement becomes unrelated custom integration;
- customers can solve the problem through a routine ERP upgrade;
- the audit has no measurable business consequence;
- data-access and security overhead exceed the likely contract value;
- no customer progresses toward production or recurring payment.

## 21. Recommended launch version

For the first three customers:

- charge €500–€1,000 plus VAT;
- limit scope to one export and three recipients where possible;
- request explicit access to finance and technical stakeholders;
- require anonymized artifacts before the clock starts;
- deliver within ten business days;
- credit the fee toward a tightly scoped pilot;
- track founder time in detail;
- turn every repeated mapping, rule, and diagnostic into a reusable internal asset.

The aim is not to maximize audit revenue. The aim is to discover a repeatable, valuable production workflow while proving that qualified customers will pay before the full FakturaPass platform is built.
